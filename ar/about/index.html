01 About Mawid

Built for the Gulf, by people who understand it.

Every booking platform in the GCC was built somewhere else, then translated. Fresha is global. Booksy is global. Boonus and Locale tried to localize. None of them were engineered ground-up for the way Saudi, Emirati, Qatari, and Kuwaiti businesses actually run. Mawid is.

02 The story

Why Mawid exists.

Anyone who has tried to book a salon, clinic, or fitness studio in Riyadh, Dubai, Doha, or Kuwait City knows the drill. You DM them on Instagram. They ask for your phone number. You wait for a WhatsApp confirmation that may or may not come. You show up and hope your time is still booked.

On the other side of that conversation, the salon owner is running fourteen WhatsApp threads, one calendar in Google Sheets, another in their head, and a stack of paper notes for color formulas and customer preferences. No-shows hit twenty to thirty percent. Reactivation campaigns are nonexistent. Every booking is a manual reconstruction.

The global platforms can't fix this — they don't speak Arabic dialects, don't render Hijri dates, don't speak the Gulf customer's language, and don't understand that Friday morning is for prayer. The local platforms can't fix it either — they're underbuilt, with weak product and no investment.

Mawid is the platform we wished existed. Native to four GCC markets from day one. WhatsApp-first, because that's where bookings actually happen. Arabic-first, with per-country dialect support in our AI assistant. Hijri and Gregorian calendars rendered together. A searchable customer database that remembers every visit, every preference, every name.

03 What we believe

Four principles we don't compromise on.

These aren't aspirational poster slogans. Every product decision either lines up with these or gets cut.

01

Native, not translated.

We don't ship a global product with Arabic strings glued on. The data layer is bilingual at the row level. The AI replies in Najdi, Emirati, Qatari, or Kuwaiti — not just modern standard Arabic. The calendar renders Hijri dates because that's how Friday is scheduled in Riyadh.

02

WhatsApp before everything.

Eighty-five percent of GCC consumers transact daily on WhatsApp. They will not download your app. They will not remember a password. Every customer-facing flow we build starts with the question: does this work in WhatsApp first? If not, we redesign it.

03

Honest pricing, no hidden fees.

We charge a flat subscription plus a small platform fee on online payments — published on the pricing page. No surcharges on SMS, no extra fees for support, no markup on payment processing. Salons see exactly what they're paying.

04

Ship, then iterate, with real customers.

We're opening with fifty private-beta businesses across Riyadh, Dubai, Abu Dhabi, Doha, and Kuwait City. Not for marketing optics — because the only way to build a product that works for Gulf service businesses is to ship it to them, watch what breaks, and fix it.

04 The company

Diyar Holdings.

Mawid is operated by Diyar Holdings LLC, a Wyoming-registered company purpose-built to scale technology products into Gulf markets. We chose Wyoming for the legal infrastructure — clean US incorporation, banking, and payment processing — while operating natively in the GCC where our customers are.

Our team has shipped consumer and enterprise products across the Gulf, North America, and Australia. We've built modular housing across UAE and Saudi Arabia, wellness hardware in fifty US states, and digital marketing infrastructure for service businesses. Mawid is what happens when that operational and technical experience focuses on a single, sharp problem: how Gulf service businesses run their day.
